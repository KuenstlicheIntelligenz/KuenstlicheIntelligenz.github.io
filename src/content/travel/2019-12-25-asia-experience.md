# My Trip to Asia and What Not to Do Again

## TL/DR - Lessons Learned
- Don't lie down under the cold stream of the ventilation system
- Be sure to have the right conversion rate in mind
- Sending items back home may be expensive - plan ahead
- Locals pay max $200 for a faked Honda Win in good condition
- Change money only at banks - check rates first
- Always clarify prices beforehand, no matter how awkward it feels
- Learn bargaining and start with at least half the asking price
- Get a bag with waist strap!

## The Journey

I made this trip with two university friends - Dr. Frank and Alan. Good travel companions are essential for longer trips, and three weeks is the minimum I'd recommend for Asia.

### Flight & Beijing Layover

Our journey started with a flight from Düsseldorf to Beijing with an ~8 hour layover. Pro tip: don't sleep directly under airport ventilation. Instead of moving 10 meters away, we stubbornly burrowed deeper under our jackets.

### Bangkok - First Impressions

The connecting flight to Bangkok was delayed, leaving us just one afternoon to explore. At the ATM, I had my first "big brain" moment - withdrew 33,000 Baht instead of 3,000 (roughly €1,000). That was going to be an expensive night...

The endless stream of Tuk-Tuks and taxis started immediately. We took our first Tuk-Tuk ride - a three-wheeled metal cage with exposed engine and DIY brake lights. No seatbelts, overtaking on both sides. For rule-habitual Europeans, Asian traffic is a near-death experience. But you learn to let go and trust the flow. That's the beauty of travel - it forces you to grow.

### Khao San Road

Alan insisted Khao San Road was a must-see. It's chaotic, loud, and overcrowded - a party street where everyone wants to sell you something. Like walking through tall grass in Pokémon, constantly attacked by vendors.

Many blogs claim it's great for meeting backpackers. I disagree. You go once to see it, then leave as soon as possible.

### DaNang to Hoi An

We flew to DaNang International Airport, then took a taxi to Hoi An (1 hour). Hoi An is famous for its tailors, so naturally we had suits made.

Here's where we hit another trap: most backpackers travel south to north, but we went in reverse. This meant we couldn't carry extra luggage and had to mail items home. The tailor said "no problem" - then hit us with the shipping cost. Add half the suit's price on top. Not expensive in absolute terms since suits were cheap, but definitely surprising.

They "helpfully" faked our invoice so we wouldn't have to pay import taxes at home. 😉

### Honda Win Adventures

We tried buying Honda Wins (motorcycles). A local took us through narrow passageways to someone selling bikes. I failed at bargaining and couldn't lower the $280 price for a fake. Alan had better luck buying from another backpacker.

## Travel Tips Summary

**Money:**
- Know conversion rates before withdrawing
- Change money only at official banks
- Compare rates before exchanging
- Only change small amounts at a time

**Negotiating:**
- Always ask prices upfront
- Start bargaining at 50% of asking price
- Don't be shy about negotiating

**Logistics:**
- Plan shipping costs before buying
- Get proper luggage with waist straps
- Consider your travel direction

**Survival:**
- Don't fight Asian traffic - embrace it
- Skip tourist traps like Khao San Road
- Trust your travel companions
